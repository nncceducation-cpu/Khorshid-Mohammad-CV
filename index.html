Overview

Khorshid Mohammad is a neonatologist and Clinical Professor of Pediatrics at the University of Calgary. In 2014 he established the Neonatal Neuro-Critical Care program at Alberta Children's Hospital, one of two such programs in Canada, and he now directs the Harvey Sarnat Neonatal Neuro-Critical Care Fellowship. His clinical and research work connects bedside neonatal neurocritical care with neonatal hemodynamics and brain monitoring. It also reaches into quality improvement, medical education, and the practical use of artificial intelligence in clinical care. He is a founding member of the Sonographic Clinical Assessment of the Newborn program and of the Newborn Brain Society, where he chairs the Education Committee, and he led the Canadian effort to establish neonatal neurocritical care as a recognized area of training.

Education and Innovation

  • Harvey Sarnat NNCC Fellowship platform. Designed and built the fellowship website end to end. It carries a competency-based curriculum of thirteen four-week blocks mapped to the seven CanMEDS roles, a members' portal and learning-management system with graded assignments and completion certificates, a learning-modules library with a large question bank, a Smart Tools section, and the SarnatNNCC YouTube channel.

Curriculum and knowledge translation

  • Neonatal neurocritical care curriculum. Developer of CME-accredited online modules (54 credits) in neonatal neurology, used for training and long-term mentorship internationally.
  • Newborn Brain Society education. Chair of the Education Committee, leading a weekly CME webinar series running since 2020, alongside the Small Brains, Big Dreams podcast produced with the Canadian Premature Babies Foundation.
  • Cranial Ultrasound Course. Organizer of hands-on cranial ultrasonography, aEEG and EEG, and neuromonitoring workshops locally, nationally, and internationally.
  • AI in Your White Coat Pocket. Designer of a 16-week, 112 CME-hour accredited course on applied artificial intelligence for clinicians, enrolling more than 800 participants from 48 countries.

Impact and reach

  • Neuroprotection in HIE. A provincial care pathway, 24/7 continuous video EEG monitoring by trained bedside nurses, and a neuro-exam certification process were associated with a fall in HIE-related mortality from 13 percent to 5 percent and a fall in brain injury on MRI from 48 percent to 33 percent.
  • Drive IVH to Zero. A seven-year quality improvement program for infants under 29 weeks achieved a sustained reduction of more than 50 percent in severe intraventricular hemorrhage.
  • Online neonatal education. The CME program (172 RCPSC credits) reached more than 2,200 learners across 148 countries, with over 15,000 cumulative course enrollments.
  • Neonatal Brain Injury book. More than 600,000 downloads across 158 countries.

Research Profile

Focus areas and active research programs.

The research program joins neonatal hemodynamics and targeted neonatal echocardiography with neonatal neurocritical care. A central thread is inotrope stewardship, the finding that the treatment of hypotension, rather than hypotension itself, can carry the greater risk of brain injury. Related work covers neuroprotection care bundles for premature and asphyxiated infants, cranial ultrasound classification, brain perfusion and monitoring, and the application of quality improvement and artificial intelligence to bedside care.

Active research programs

  • Cerebral edema timing in hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy. Serial cranial ultrasonography and transcranial Doppler to identify peak cerebral edema in neonates undergoing therapeutic hypothermia. ACHRI Clinical Research Fellowship application in preparation.
  • Economic evaluation of the first-72-hour neuroprotection care bundle. Model-based cost-effectiveness analysis of the Calgary neuroprotection bundle for infants born before 29 weeks. First author, manuscript under revision.
